✅ Financial Recovery Roadmap

Work these in order. Progress saves privately in this browser only.

    📄 Paperwork Navigator

    Plain-language map of the financial paperwork most divorces require. Information only — not legal advice; rules differ by place.

    Financial Affidavit / Disclosure

    The court's official snapshot of your money: income, expenses, assets, debts. You'll swear it's true — accuracy matters more than optimism. Gather 3–6 months of statements before filling anything in.

    Asset & Debt Worksheet

    Everything either of you owns or owes, usually tagged marital vs separate. Bank accounts, retirement funds, vehicles, house, credit cards, loans. Copy account numbers; never close accounts unilaterally mid-process unless advised.

    Budget / Expenses Form

    Your monthly cost of living — courts use it for support decisions. This is exactly what the calculator above helps you estimate honestly.

    Settlement Agreement

    The contract dividing property, debt and support. Mediators or document services can draft uncontested versions for far less than litigation. Read every number against your worksheets before signing.

    Support Calculations

    Child (and sometimes spousal) support usually follow statutory formulas from both incomes + parenting time. Free calculators exist for most jurisdictions — search "[your location] child support calculator".

    💳 Debt Triage

    Post-split debt is common. Order your payoffs: avalanche (highest rate first — cheapest) or snowball (smallest balance first — most motivating).

    ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

    Straight answers to the questions people type into search at 2am.

    Can I afford to get divorced?

    It depends on your income, housing options, childcare and legal route. The calculator above compares your current one-household budget against a two-household budget over 12 months — including legal fee tiers from DIY (~$500) to high-conflict ($30,000+) — so you can see the real monthly and first-year numbers before deciding.

    How much does a divorce cost?

    Costs vary widely: DIY online filings can run a few hundred dollars, mediated or uncontested divorces average around $5,000, and contested cases with lawyers routinely exceed $15,000–$30,000. Beyond legal fees, the bigger long-term cost is usually running two households instead of one.

    Is SplitCost really private?

    Yes. SplitCost is a static website with no server, no accounts and no analytics. Every calculation runs in your own browser, and nothing you type is ever transmitted or stored anywhere except optional checklist progress in your own device's local storage.

    Is SplitCost financial or legal advice?

    No. SplitCost is a free educational planning tool. It gives you estimates based on the numbers you enter so you can prepare for consultations with qualified professionals — it does not replace legal, financial or tax advice.

    Does SplitCost cost anything?

    No — it's completely free and open source (MIT license). There are no accounts, subscriptions or ads.
